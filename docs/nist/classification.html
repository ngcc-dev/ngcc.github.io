NIST additional-signature report classification

This policy is specific to the NIST additional digital signature process. It uses the required EUF-CMA model, claimed security categories, and evaluation criteria on the official NIST call page and in the call PDF. The call permits security estimates to assume at most 2^64 chosen-message signing queries. Ratings here are independent project assessments, not ratings issued by NIST. NGCC findings may supply prior evidence, but their ratings do not transfer automatically.

Each numbered report carries a Credit attribution and independent Severity, Status, Layer, Discovery, Exploitation, and Remediation fields. These use the same classification axes as the NGCC study, but severity is assessed against the NIST additional-signature call and the submission's own claims. An earlier NGCC rating is evidence, not a rating that automatically transfers here.

Ratings describe cryptanalytic impact on the affected scheme and parameters:

Rating Interpretation
Critical Full key recovery or general forgery invalidates the intended security target; state query/work requirements and whether demonstrated or estimated.
High A serious failure short of a required-property break: for example, a well-supported recovery/forgery path with one material gap, an end-to-end side-channel recovery without an explicit resistance claim, or failure of a claimed SUF-CMA property when EUF-CMA remains intact.
Medium Confirmed repeated or measured leakage without recovery, a material constant-time rewrite, a demonstrated failure of an unclaimed stronger security property such as SUF-CMA, or a substantive binding/robustness defect whose attack consequence is unresolved.
Low Source-level secret dependence without a measured channel when a local repair is known, or another narrow security-relevant robustness/API weakness.
Info Historical context, a withdrawn claim, or an observation without an established attack on the evaluated submission version.

A Critical rating does not mean every parameter can be attacked cheaply. The result, concrete costs, scope and confidence must accompany the rating. These are project assessments under the NIST evaluation baseline, not CVSS scores or NIST-issued ratings. Strong-unforgeability, side-channel or fault models are distinguished from the required EUF-CMA model.

A candidate-specific proof gap is normally Low when a reproduced calculation shows that the submitted reduction becomes ineffective within NIST's stated evaluation budget, but no attack follows. Generic looseness without a concrete boundary is Informational. A limitation that the submission itself identifies as a proof artifact remains Informational only when it is generic looseness without a reproduced concrete boundary. A candidate-specific calculation that makes the submitted reduction ineffective within NIST's stated evaluation budget is Low even when the submission acknowledges the loss and no attack follows. High requires a supported recovery or forgery path with a material remaining gap; failure of a proof alone is not High.

The 2^64 chosen-message figure is an estimation assumption, not a hard boundary on relevant attacks. NIST's call §4.B.2 (physical p.14) says that attacks using more messages may also be considered and that NIST is primarily concerned with classical, rather than quantum, queries to the signing oracle. Reports therefore state both the query count and query model; crossing 2^64 does not by itself make an attack irrelevant, and a quantum-query reduction is not presented as the call's primary signing-oracle model.

A concrete security-margin calculation may be a Low / Lead without a demonstrated attack when it is candidate-specific, reproducible from a primary model, and leaves less than two bits of margin under that model while a documented unresolved modeling choice could erase the margin. The report must identify the omitted cost, memory assumption, or heuristic and must not present the calculation as an attack. A model-dependent observation without such a concrete boundary is Informational.

Status describes evidence strength, independently of severity:

  • Confirmed: source/specification reasoning, a mathematical certificate, or a controlled reproducible experiment directly establishes the exact reported behavior. This does not imply end-to-end exploitation.
  • Probable: the mechanism and consequence are strongly supported, but an important source, platform, or experimental step remains unchecked.
  • Lead: a plausible candidate-specific attack still depends on a material unvalidated assumption or experiment.
  • Proof gap: a claimed theorem, reduction, or bound is insufficient, but an attack of the claimed strength has not been established.
  • Withdrawn: the stable report is false, duplicate, out of scope, or superseded. Withdrawn reports have Informational severity. A withdrawn candidate or claim is recorded separately from report status.

Layer identifies the root cause:

  • Design: the normative construction, parameter choice, proof, or advertised portable performance profile contains the weakness.
  • Implementation: submitted code introduces an API, parser, memory-safety, arithmetic, or randomness defect not required by the design.
  • Side-channel: the finding depends on a timing, branch, cache, division, power, fault, or other execution trace and does not meet the Design rule.
  • Evaluation: the issue is confined to KAT, archive, build, provenance, or other evaluation material.

Discovery records the reasoning needed to identify and substantiate the mechanism, rather than severity or attack runtime: Trivial for direct local inspection; Moderate for focused review or a standard attack pattern; Non-trivial for a multi-step candidate-specific argument; and Hard for substantial new cryptanalytic insight or engineering.

Remediation may be None for an accurate historical or withdrawn-claim record that requires no change to the evaluated submission.

Exploitation is a short factual boundary such as source-only, measured-channel, candidate-specific path, or end-to-end. It states the strongest completed step and the observation model. Remediation is None, Local, Moderate, or Architectural, describing respectively no change for an accurate historical record; a small constant-time/API repair; a material implementation replacement or proof/parameter reassessment that preserves the construction and API; or a change to the construction, API, or advertised performance profile.

Constant-time classification

Constant-time surveys distinguish public control flow from operations on a secret or secret-derived operand. A report answers, in order: what is secret; who can observe the trace; whether a channel was measured and repeated; whether observations have a candidate-specific map to a key or forgery; and whether that map was executed end to end. Source-level review alone is not a timing measurement or a constant-time certification.

Evidence and consequence Usual severity
Secret branch/address in source, no measured channel, local repair Low
Repeated secret leakage, measured channel, or material rewrite; no recovery Medium
Fine-grained leakage intrinsic to an advertised fast path with no supplied or known competitive portable constant-time realization High may be justified
Stable oracle and candidate-specific recovery path with one channel or scale gap High
End-to-end recovery/forgery without an explicit resistance claim High
End-to-end recovery/forgery contradicting an explicit relevant resistance claim Critical

Remediation does not mechanically set severity. Local normally supports Low; a Moderate rewrite supports Medium only when the report independently establishes repeated finer-grained leakage and explains why the affected trace must be hidden. Architectural remediation may support High for an intrinsic advertised fast path. A costly repair alone does not raise source-only secret dependence above Low, particularly when the specification treats the observed predicate as public or simulatable. Public-indexed tables, branches on public lengths, and verifier rejection paths are not secret leaks. The absence of a hardened implementation by itself does not prove a design defect.

Entropy and malleability classification

The survey traces key-generation and signing entropy from the NIST randombytes boundary to all scheme-specific expanders and salts. Seed replay in KAT testing is a control, not an attacker capability. Deterministic signing or a signer that deliberately ignores fresh external coins is an observation until the specification, proof, nonce-reuse behavior, or a demonstrated attack makes it security relevant. Entropy estimates count reachable states under the scheme's actual derivation and domain separation rather than just buffer widths.

NIST's call §4.B.4 (physical p.18) identifies resistance to isolated coding errors, random-number-generator malfunction, and nonce reuse as a desirable property, rather than a minimum acceptability requirement. This project rates a break that requires one exact repeat of the external signing-RNG output Informational when the submission makes no explicit hedged-signing or randomness-reuse-resistance claim. The report still records a demonstrated key recovery or fresh-message forgery and its precise operational consequence. Low or a stronger rating requires an additional candidate-specific trigger in the claimed operating model, a contradiction of an explicit robustness claim, or a break that does not require RNG failure. FAEST cross-profile reuse remains Low because its trigger includes the specified deterministic-signing mode and missing profile separation, rather than only an external RNG malfunction.

Malleability tests distinguish (1) a modified signed message that verifies to the same message, which is relevant to SUF-CMA or canonical encoding, from (2) a modification that verifies to a different message, which is a direct EUF-CMA concern. Sampled mutation, truncation, extension, zero-input, and wrong-key tests provide negative evidence only for the tested cases. A demonstrated efficient transformation of a valid signature into a distinct valid signature on the same message is normally Medium even when the submission does not claim SUF-CMA: it is an end-to-end break of a commonly used additional signature property, while leaving NIST's required EUF-CMA target intact. Failure of an explicit SUF-CMA claim is normally High. An EUF-CMA forgery is Critical when it defeats the claimed category.
