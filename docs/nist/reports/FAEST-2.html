FAEST-2 — Low: ROM and QROM reductions miss claimed-category guarantees at the NIST signing-query baseline

Bound and concrete substitution

Corollary 10.54 (physical p. 139) permits up to Q_sig = 2^64 signing-oracle queries and includes the nonnegative additive term

6 Q_sig sqrt(ceil(log2 L) AdvPRP_PRG2lambda[1]
             + AdvPRP_LeafCommit[1]
             + 3 sqrt(Q1) 2^-lambda
             + Q2,1 2^-2lambda).

The first two primitive advantages are one-query advantages. Under the standard ideal-primitive key-search substitution, each is at least about 2^-lambda: an adversary guesses the keyed primitive's lambda-bit key and tests it with one query. Dropping the other nonnegative terms therefore gives

6 Q_sig sqrt((ceil(log2 L) + 1) 2^-lambda).

At Q_sig = 2^64, this term alone is between 2^4.44 and 2^4.58 for the four lambda=128 parameter sets. The right-hand side of the reduction is therefore already greater than one under the one-key-guess substitution and provides no concrete upper bound on forgery probability for those four sets at NIST's signing-query baseline.

The lambda=128 conclusion does not depend on the primitive substitution. Keeping only the statistical summand 3 sqrt(Q1) 2^-lambda inside the same square root gives 6 sqrt(3) Q1^(1/4) >= 2^3.38 for any adversary making at least one H1 query. Independently, the corollary's EUF-KO bound contains 10 Q^2 (tau + dzk) 2^-lambda (FAEST) or 10 Q^2 tau 2^-lambda (FAEST-EM), multiplied by (1 - dom/cod)^-1 = 4 at lambda=128, with Q >= (tau+4) Qsig. At Qsig = 2^64 and no adversarial hash queries, twice that term is between 2^17.6 and 2^19.7 for the four lambda=128 sets, and it stays below one only for Qsig below about 2^54.2 to 2^55.2.

For the higher categories, use the ordinary Grover key-search model AdvPRP(t) = t^2 / 2^lambda for both primitive terms. The same additive term reaches one at only the following quantum-work exponents:

lambda short profiles fast profiles ideal key-search exponent
192 27.37 27.51 96
256 59.37 59.51 128

The small difference between short and fast profiles comes from their values of L. Thus Corollary 10.54 also fails to certify the claimed-category quantum work at the full signing-query baseline for the 192- and 256-bit sets. The submission describes its QROM bound as giving meaningful concrete security on physical p. 4 and describes the proofs as almost tight apart from signing query loss on physical p. 140. “Vacuous” here applies directly to the lambda=128 bounds at Q_sig = 2^64; for lambda=192 and lambda=256, the calculation instead shows that the reduction reaches one at quantum work far below the claimed-category target.

Classical ROM bound

The classical-ROM Theorem 10.42 (physical p. 115) has the same per-signature hybrid structure without the square root. Substituting the generic one-key advantage t/2^lambda for each of its 3 + (tau+1) ceil(log2 L) single-key PRF/PRP terms, the bound reaches one at t = 2^55.2 to 2^55.5, 2^118.6 to 2^118.9 and 2^182.2 to 2^182.5 for the 128-, 192- and 256-bit sets at Qsig = 2^64. This is NIST's primary model (classical signing queries), and the shortfall is about 73 bits at every level. The design survey recorded this as DS-FAEST-2 (Informational); it has the same root cause and is folded into this report. The submission's per-signature IVs and per-node tweaks argue against multi-target key search (§10.1.2, physical p. 72), so the loss is presumably a proof artifact that an ideal-cipher treatment of the PRG would remove.

Classification and limits

This is a Low / Proof gap because it is a candidate-specific shortfall in explicit concrete ROM and QROM claims. It is not an attack. The calculation does not construct a FAEST forgery, recover a key, or show that the parameter sets have the displayed work factors in practice. It only shows that the stated reductions cannot establish their claimed ROM and QROM category guarantees at 2^64 signing queries under the standard primitive substitutions.

The substitution reads AdvPRP[1] as the usual best one-query advantage. For lambda=192 and lambda=256, if those symbols are instead restricted to only the particular reduction adversaries constructed in the proof, Corollary 10.54 supplies no concrete upper bounds for those advantages; a claimed-category concrete guarantee still does not follow from the displayed corollary. For lambda=128 the bound exceeds one from its statistical terms alone. The submission's QROM summary on physical p. 127 (§10.11.4) describes the loss as a multiplicative factor of about 8 and omits the Qsig factor. Closing the gap requires tighter ROM and QROM reductions, additional concrete bounds for the reduction adversaries, or an explicit usage limit included in the security claim.

Reproduction

python3 FAEST/reproduce_faest_2.py

The deterministic script checks the pinned specification hash and repository NIST baseline, reads all twelve extracted L values, and recomputes the one-key-guess and Grover substitutions, the lambda=128 statistical and EUF-KO terms, and the classical-ROM thresholds above. This is a cost/model check of the stated reduction, not a cryptanalytic attack.

Harness reproduction

From the ngcc-harness checkout, run the pinned source script through the harness-side dispatcher:

python3 nist/reproduce.py FAEST-2
