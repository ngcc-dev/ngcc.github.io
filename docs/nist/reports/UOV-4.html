UOV-4 — Low: selective divided-power rows conditionally put uov-V below target

Result

The submission's §4.6 truncated-polynomial-ring calculation remains a useful baseline. Recomputing it with exact integer power series gives:

Parameter set Specification-formula cost Claimed reference Margin
uov-Ip 144.75 143 +1.75
uov-Is 158.55 143 +15.55
uov-III 213.12 207 +6.12
uov-V 272.05 272 +0.05

Table 5 floors these logarithms to 144, 158, 213 and 272. The baseline puts no set below its claim.

Merz and Ran's divided-power Schur--Macaulay attack constructs several families of linear equations in the oil-space Pluecker coordinates and uses block Wiedemann to find the one-dimensional kernel. Their §6.1 cost estimate randomly shortens the rectangular matrix to a square one and charges the average row density. The full-shape estimates are about 143.9, 158.1, 216.6 and 280.7 bits for uov-Ip, uov-Is, uov-III and uov-V.

If the plain rows have their predicted rank, an independent subset can keep all but its nullity from that cheap family and use denser divided-square rows only to supply the missing rank. Under the partial-tower rank assumption described below, the same RAM-gate convention gives:

Parameter set Selective-row cost Random partial-family cost Target Margin
uov-Ip no admissible instance no admissible instance 143 --
uov-Is 153.43 155.68 143 +10.43
uov-III 209.26 211.75 207 +2.26
uov-V 267.58 271.32 272 -4.42

For uov-V the valid route first restricts the odd-dimensional public system to a generic hyperplane and then applies the characteristic-two intersection: (v,o,m)=(163,96,96) becomes (163,95,96) and then (v',o',m')=(231,27,286). At degree 27 the model has log2(N)=121.1327, predicted plain-family nullity log2(h0)=110.4417, plain row weight C(233,2)=27028, and divided-square row weight C(235,4)=123855810. Selecting N-h0+1 cheap rows and h0-1 dense rows gives log2(3*nnz*N*136)=267.5751.

The previously proposed 266.8-bit uov-V and 140.7-bit uov-Ip values are not admissible. They apply the characteristic-two intersection directly at odd ambient dimension, where the alternating matrices that must be inverted are singular. Hyperplane restriction preserves the uov-V lead but eliminates the uov-Ip instance. It also moves uov-III to 209.26, above its target.

Unresolved rank premise

The calculation assumes the partial divided-power series

(1+t)^n / ((1+t^2)^m (1+t^4)^m)
  = (1+t)^n (1-t^2)^m / (1-t^8)^m

predicts the ranks of the plain and divided-square row families at the selected degree. Merz--Ran conjecture and test the full divided-power tower; they do not establish this truncated-family series or sparse-first selection at the uov-V dimensions. Their paper also warns that systems produced by the intersection construction need not behave as generic systems.

The retained Sage witness finds a counterexample to the partial formula as a general identity. Over GF(256), a planted UOV instance with (v,a,m)=(4,10,1) has N=1001. The formula predicts ranks 911 and 1000 for the plain and combined families, but three seeded trials give ranks 895 and 959. This instance has more oil than vinegar and only one form; even the plain rank prediction fails. It does not model the uov-V ratio. A second planted toy with (v,a,m)=(9,8,3) also misses the predicted plain rank (23680 versus 24193), although its plain-plus-divided-square family reaches corank one.

Independent finite-field toys strengthen the positive evidence. Planted toys with v/a at least 1.43 and small intersection-derived UOV toys over GF(16) and GF(256) matched the predicted plain and combined ranks. Selected square submatrices reached corank one, with the oil Pluecker vector as their kernel. The intersection-derived toys used random linear combinations of only part of the 3m-2 transformed form family, however. They reached degree at most 8, versus degree 27 and v'/a=231/27 for uov-V. Neither the full transformed family nor the submitted dimensions have been checked. Merz--Ran's published conjecture covers the full divided-power tower for generic systems, not this partial family after the intersection transform.

Conditional on the two required ranks, keeping the cheap rows is legitimate: linear algebra guarantees a basis containing N-h0 independent plain rows and h0-1 additional divided-square rows. Dropping rows can create extra kernel vectors unless the selected matrix still has rank N-1. Small toys confirm this rank for their instances; a practical full-scale selection procedure and the rank premise itself remain unchecked. Oversampling the divided-square rows by factors of 2 to 20 gives conditional uov-V estimates of 268.4 to 271.5 bits. Random selection from only the plain and divided-square families gives 271.32 bits under the same rank assumption, just 0.68 bit below target.

Memory and interpretation

The block-Wiedemann vector alone contains N GF(256) elements, about 2^124.13 bits for uov-V. Storing the selected sparse matrix needs at least about 2^140.77 bits for its nonzero field elements, before indices and overhead; regenerating rows trades storage for work. This report has not recomputed the submission's MEM-2D model for the selective matrix, so the 267.58 result is only a RAM-gate figure. A mesh-style sensitivity estimate is above target but is not presented as an official NIST-category cost. NIST's call, §4.B.3 allows multiple resource metrics, including the cost of access to extremely large memories; a RAM-only shortfall is therefore not a settled category failure.

The 2N or 1.1N iteration variants discussed in Chou and Niederhagen are not used in the stated result. Their applicability and setup costs for this Pluecker-kernel problem have not been established; even the optimistic iteration count does not put the corrected uov-III construction clearly below target.

This is retained as Low / Lead, rather than Medium / Probable. The result is candidate-specific and conditionally 4.42 bits below the primary RAM target, but the new rank premise is not a general identity, the intersection toys cover only subsets of forms at much smaller degrees, and no memory-aware category comparison or full-scale row-selection procedure exists. The random-partial estimate has less than one bit of margin. If rank, matrix generation, and an attack-specific resource cost validate a below-target path at full scale, its severity must be reassessed.

Reproduction

python3 UOV/reproduce_uov_4.py

The Python reproducer checks the specification values, applies the required odd-dimension reduction and prints the conditional selected-row arithmetic. It is a deterministic cost/model check, not a key-recovery run. With SageMath:

sage UOV/analysis/row_selection_toy.sage

The second command constructs the seeded planted-UOV counterexample and computes the exact finite-field ranks. The independent toy below constructs planted or intersection-derived systems and tests selected-row ranks; the intersection example uses twelve random combinations of the transformed forms and may take several minutes in SageMath:

sage UOV/analysis/row_selection_verifier.sage inter 8 20261018 1 11 9 5 12
