QR-UOV-1 — Medium: submitted solvers expose secret pivot structure

Secret flow and affected operations

The vinegar vector y and secret transformation sdt build the signing matrix at qruov_common.c:39-66 (QR-UOV/submission/QR_UOV - Round3/Reference_Implementation/ref/qruov_common.c#L39), and qruov_common_sign_solve_oil passes that matrix to linsys_lu_decompose at line 104. The submitted solver then:

  • searches until a nonzero secret-derived pivot is found, with early exits and secret loop counts at linsys.c:29-45 (QR-UOV/submission/QR_UOV - Round3/Reference_Implementation/ref/linsys.c#L29);
  • swaps row pointers according to that pivot at line 47;
  • indexes gf_inv_table by the pivot at lines 50–54 and gf.h:27-53 (QR-UOV/submission/QR_UOV - Round3/Reference_Implementation/ref/gf.h#L27);
  • uses the derived rank and pivot structure to set later iteration bounds and sample a solution at linsys.c:68-168; and
  • retries salt generation according to rank and consistency at qruov_common.c:104-124 (QR-UOV/submission/QR_UOV - Round3/Reference_Implementation/ref/qruov_common.c#L104).

The linsys.c and qruov_common.c files are respectively byte-identical in the reference, optimized, and AVX2 directories. No alternate constant-time solver is present in the submitted archive.

What the specification already discloses

Specification §4.10, physical p.29, states that the reference implementation does not use a constant-time linear-algebra algorithm. Appendix A treats the matrix rank as observable and analyzes that rank leak, while requiring the remaining pivot positions, values, consistency decisions, and solution assignments to use fixed ranges and mask-based updates (Appendix A.3, physical pp.65–66). The archive's solver is the original data-dependent version, not the countermeasure described there.

This report excludes the deliberately observable rank alone from its claimed defect. It covers the finer pivot row/column sequence, table indices, and data-dependent path that Appendix A itself says should be protected.

Impact and limits

A local observer can in principle distinguish secret-derived control flow and cache addresses during signing. Medium rests on the repeated fine-grained pivot sequence and table-address trace that Appendix A says should be protected, not on remediation cost alone. Replacing the solver with the specified fixed-range, mask-based procedure is a material change and corroborates that scope. No timing experiment, trace decoder, signing-key recovery, or forgery is demonstrated. The report therefore does not assert a failure of QR-UOV's claimed NIST categories or EUF-CMA security.

Reproduction

python3 QR-UOV/reproduce_qruov_1.py

The offline witness checks the exact submitted hashes in all three backends, the secret matrix flow, pivot loop, row swap, inverse table, rank retry, and absence of a second constant-time solver. It does not measure a side channel.
