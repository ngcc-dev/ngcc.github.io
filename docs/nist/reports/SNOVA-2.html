SNOVA-2 — Informational: historical block-structured wedge-product key recovery

The published estimates are High against the affected Round-2 parameter claims. This report rates the record Informational because applicability to the pinned Round-3 version is not established. Confirmed status rests on the primary paper and the SNOVA team's acknowledgement, not a local cost reproduction.

Bros, Le, Lichtinger, Minaud, Perlner, Smith-Tone and Valenzuela adapt the wedge-product method to SNOVA's block-ring structure. Their treatment removes extra/spurious solutions that obstruct a direct application of the generic UOV attack, and adds unbalanced projection of oil coordinates. The paper reports six of eleven Round-2 sets below their intended security levels, with two further sets receiving improved estimates. Its example (v,o,q,l)=(29,6,16,5) falls from the previously reported 310-bit estimate to about 181 bits. These are attack-complexity estimates, not a practical full-size key-recovery demonstration in this review. Paper 2026/237, latest listed revision September 16, 2026.

The February 24 announcement and February 25 team response agree that affected parameters require adjustment. The response links the SNOVA team's own independently conducted analysis, 2026/260, and discusses increasing vinegar dimensions. The block-structure adaptation and the team analysis concern the same attack family and are recorded together here.

A separate warning is essential when reading the history. Ran's original May 2025 generic wedge announcement included SNOVA estimates, but the June 19 follow-up withdrew those SNOVA complexities because the structured system need not have the assumed rank. The later SNOVA-specific work addresses that obstacle. The withdrawn generic estimate is not an independent confirmed SNOVA break.

The pinned v3.0 specification, §1.9.3 and §5.3.5, changes the parameter framework: r is independent of l and m1 is independent of o. Its Table 5 recommends different seven-component tuples (v,o,q,l,r,m1,m2); Table 6 also discusses alternatives. The old four-component example does not identify any current recommended instance. The specification explicitly analyzes wedge attacks, including projection, and cites this work. Those changes establish version and parameter differences; they do not independently validate every new security estimate.

There is no local full wedge-attack reproduction or demonstrated current-key recovery behind this report. The scheme KATs test functional compatibility and do not test wedge-attack resistance. Future reproduction must pin the attacked Round-2 package and field representation, include the block-ring corrections, state the cost/memory model, and compare the resulting oil subspace or a new-message forgery against a control. Running a generic UOV estimator with Round-3 labels would not reproduce the published attack.

Primary-source retrievals and failures are retained in security/harvests.jsonl; run python3 security/harvest.py --list to locate the registered wedge sources. SNOVA-1 is a distinct current-version sampling issue and must not be conflated with this historical key-only structural attack.
