MAYO-5 — Low: Lemma 2 treats an average-case restart bound as if it held for every key

Proof step

Lemma 2, step 6 (physical p.30) says that Game 5, "conditioned on Good v happening, is identical to Game 4", and concludes Pr[Game5] >= Pr[Game4] Pr[Good v] >= PrGame4 (MAYO/1 − Qs B). Here B comes from Lemma 1 (physical p.28), which bounds the probability that P*(v + ·) + L(Lambda, ·) lacks full rank on average over the oil space, the public map and the vinegar.

The identity needs the fraction of good vinegar to be the same for every key and every Lambda. Conditioning on Good v otherwise reweights keys and Lambda values by their good-vinegar fraction, and Game 5 conditioned on Good v is then not Game 4. With only an average-case bound, the step gives an additive loss Qs B, not the multiplicative factor stated.

Evidence

The reproducer checks the quoted step and Lemma 1's average-case wording on their pages of the pinned specification, then enumerates every vinegar tuple for 56,000 (key, Lambda) pairs of a toy instance over GF(2) (m = 4, o = 3, k = 2, n − o = 5) that meets the parameter rules of §2.1.1 except q.

  • The bad-vinegar fraction ranges from 0.148 to 0.336 (mean 0.2165, standard deviation 0.0128). It varies with Lambda for 13,984 of 14,000 keys, and its exact average over all Lambda still varies between keys.
  • So conditioning on Good v reweights the pairs, and Game 5 given Good v is not Game 4 on the toy.
  • Control: the exact average-case bad probability, 0.21655, is below B = 3/8, as Lemma 1 states, and the sample mean matches it.

Classification and limits

Low / Proof gap: a candidate-specific proof step that is invalid as written. No sampled pair exceeds B, so the final inequality was not shown to fail; for typical keys the good-vinegar fraction probably concentrates, and the step is likely repairable with an extra term for atypical keys. Nothing was computed at the submitted parameters (q = 16). The step is inherited from Beullens's 2022 MAYO proof. MAYO-2 is the separate limit of the same theorem to few signing queries.
