SQIsign-5 — Low: ROM bound loses security at 2^64 signing queries

Theorem 8.1.7 in the pinned specification (physical p. 106) gives SQIsign's EUF-CMA reduction in the random-oracle model. One of its nonnegative additive terms is

(q + 1)(s + 1) / 2^(lambda + 1),

where q bounds random-oracle queries and s bounds signing-oracle queries. NIST permits up to s = 2^64 chosen-message signing queries when estimating security. With that value, this term reaches one by q = 2^(lambda-63): approximately 2^65, 2^129, and 2^193 oracle queries for the three parameter sets. These query exponents are far below the corresponding 143-, 207-, and 272-gate category thresholds.

The term alone is about one half for the Level-I set when both q and s are 2^64; it exceeds one at roughly 2^65 random-oracle queries. For Levels III and V, its respective thresholds are roughly 2^129 and 2^193. Once the term reaches one, the theorem's right-hand side supplies no nontrivial upper bound on forgery probability. The separate final min-entropy term is

(q + s + 1)s / (sqrt(p) 2^lambda).

At q = 2^lambda and s = 2^64, its base-2 logarithm is approximately -98.8, -188.4, and -270.0 for the three parameter sets. It therefore remains small under the Round-3 primes and does not repair this statistical-distance loss.

The theorem is stated only in the classical ROM and is accompanied by a proof sketch based on an earlier Fiat–Shamir-with-hints theorem. Remark 8.1.8 obtains a QROM statement only in the Algebraic Isogeny Model. Section 1.1's statement that the reduction is tight is expressly qualified on accepting standard heuristics; the query-dependent term above still limits the concrete guarantee available from the displayed theorem.

This is Low / Proof gap. It does not give an attack: no signature is forged, no key is recovered, and the calculation does not estimate SQIsign's actual forgery advantage. It shows only that Theorem 8.1.7 does not certify the claimed concrete security through NIST's signing-query baseline. A tighter reduction or an explicit, accepted per-key usage limit is needed to close it.

Recompute the terms with:

python3 SQIsign/reproduce_sqisign_5.py

The deterministic script checks the pinned specification and NIST baseline, computes the exact first integer q that makes the term at least one, and checks the final min-entropy term for every Round-3 prime. It is a proof-bound calculation, not an attack.
