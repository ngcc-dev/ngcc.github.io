MAYO-4 — Low: Round 3 linear term turns an all-zero-vinegar fault into valid signatures in the oil space

Prior work

Vinegar faults on MAYO are known. Aulbach, Campos and Krämer (SoK: On the Physical Security of UOV-based Signature Schemes, PQCrypto 2025, §3.3) note that a fault leaving "one or more (but not all)" vinegar vectors zero still gives a solvable system, so the corresponding oil vectors appear in the signature. Jendral and Dubrova (MAYO Key Recovery by Fixing Vinegar Seeds, CIC 2025) make the vinegar seed predictable by voltage glitching and recover the key from a faulty signature; Sayari et al. and Aulbach et al. describe loop-abort and skipped-sampling variants. These attacks target earlier MAYO versions without the linear term.

This report covers the remaining case: all vinegar vectors zero. Before Round 3 that attempt gives a zero system and fails, so the signer retries; in Round 3 it succeeds.

Mechanism

Round 3 adds the linear term L(Lambda, s) to the verification equation (Algorithm 7, physical p.15). If every vinegar vector of a signing attempt is zero, all quadratic contributions vanish and the attempt's linear system is sum_i E_i0 (Lambda_v O + Lambda_o) x_i = t. That system is full rank about as often as an honest one, so the attempt succeeds, and each signature block s_i then lies in the secret oil space. The signature verifies, so verifying after signing does not detect the fault. In the Round 2 design, without the linear term, the same system is zero and the attempt fails.

The vinegar is derived at mayo.c:496-497 (MAYO/submission/MAYO/Reference_Implementation/src/mayo.c#L496) and folded into the system through m_calculate_M_and_VPV (arithmetic.h:262 (MAYO/submission/MAYO/Reference_Implementation/src/arithmetic.h#L262)).

Evidence

The reproducer builds MAYO1, 2, 3 and 5 from the reference sources, with a temporary copy of mayo.c that adds one switchable memset zeroing the vinegar after the SHAKE call. The fault is simulated in software.

Set Faulty signer succeeded Verified by unmodified open Signatures for exact recovery of O
MAYO1 276/300 276/276 1 in every run
MAYO2 300/300 300/300 3 (k = 5 < o = 13)
MAYO3 300/300 300/300 1 in 296 of 298 runs, otherwise 2
MAYO5 300/300 300/300 1 in 256 of 278 runs, otherwise 2

Every signature block lies in the oil space of the key, and row reduction of the blocks reproduces O exactly. Controls: with Lambda also zeroed on the signer side (Round 2 behaviour) every faulty call fails; unfaulted signatures verify and are not in the oil space; with both switches off the patched build is byte-identical to the unmodified one.

Classification and limits

Low / Confirmed / Side-channel (fault model). It extends the known vinegar-fault attacks to the all-zero case that earlier MAYO versions handled safely. Fault attacks are outside EUF-CMA; the specification says the salt "brings some protection" against fault attacks (physical p.31). No physical fault was attempted, and partial faults (some vinegar vectors zero, or known non-zero vinegar) were not re-tested here; they are covered by the prior work above.

A local check that rejects an attempt whose vinegar is zero addresses this exact fault; general fault resistance needs the usual countermeasures, such as re-deriving the vinegar or checking that a signature does not lie in the oil space.
