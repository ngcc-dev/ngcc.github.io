MAYO-3 — Low: MAYO2 structural-forgery estimate depends on cost accounting

Result

Ostuzzi's September 2026 paper Geometric Forgeries: Structural Cryptanalysis of MAYO gives a new structural direct-forgery strategy for On-Ramp Round 3 MAYO. Its Table 6 reports 140 bits for MAYO2, against the submitters' Category-1 target of 143 bit operations. An independent reimplementation of the paper's Theorems 1 and 3 and Section 7.2 dimension model reproduced the estimate as 2^140.8 bit operations. The same reimplementation reproduced all four printed On-Ramp Round 3 figures to within 0.8 bit.

The paper's result depends on how a field operation and per-guess work are priced:

Model MAYO2 log2 bit operations
Paper convention: 16 bit operations per GF(16) operation 140.8
Submission convention: 36 bit operations per GF(16) operation 142.2
Submission convention with cubic matrix arithmetic 142.9
Paper convention with symmetric specialization accounting 145.1
Submission convention with symmetric specialization accounting 146.3

The submission uses 36 bit operations per GF(16) multiply-add and states that known-attack estimates should exceed 2^143 for Category 1 (§5.4). On that like-for-like convention, the estimate is about 0.8 bit below the target, rather than the roughly 3-bit shortfall under the paper's 16-bit-operation convention. The result is insensitive to changing the matrix exponent from 2.81 to 3: the corresponding value is 142.9.

Specialization-cost sensitivity

The paper author's generic baseline charges a per-guess specialization cost, but the structural On-Ramp Round 3 model omits that cost. Applying the same generic specialization formula to the structural schedule raises the estimate to 145.1 under the paper convention and 146.3 under the submission convention. The optimizing block schedule also changes from four blocks of size two to two blocks of size four.

This sensitivity calculation is not a proof that the higher values are the right attack cost. Gray-code enumeration may amortize some of the specialization work. Conversely, the submission's own direct-attack estimate also says that its methodology ignores the sometimes non-negligible cost of variable substitutions and eliminations (§5.4). The defensible range therefore crosses the 143-bit target, depending on a presently unsettled accounting choice.

Evidence, classification, and limits

For the paper convention, the selected MAYO2 schedule has a=5, u=15, r=15, w=40, h=46, and four blocks of size two. Its two dominant terms are approximately 2^140.1 for the endpoint iterations and 2^139.0 for the small-system solves. The strategy is readily parallelized and does not depend on the large-memory model that limits some other multivariate attacks.

The paper supplies no experiments. Its estimate assumes random-system heuristics and treats dimension bounds as equalities. This study did not run a forgery attack, and the symmetric-cost estimate moves the result above the claimed target. The finding is therefore Low / Lead: it records a narrow, model-dependent concrete-margin concern, not a demonstrated Category-1 break. Experimental validation of the dimension assumptions and a consistent account of per-guess specialization would determine whether the lead survives.

Reproduction

python3 MAYO/reproduce_mayo_3.py

The deterministic standard-library script pins the On-Ramp Round 3 specification, recomputes the selected dimensions, verifies the exact specialization operation count, and checks all five estimates above from the independently verified cost certificates. It records the verified full-paper SHA-256. It is a selected-cost certificate, not a repeat of the external-estimator exhaustive search, an experiment, or an attack execution.
