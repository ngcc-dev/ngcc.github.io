SQIsign-4 — Low: validated free-memory attack model falls below category targets

SQIsign's pinned specification incorporates the p^(1/3+o(1)) supersingular endomorphism-ring attack. Section 1.1 (physical pp. 4--5) calls its concrete implications uncertain, notes its exponential memory, and describes the parameters as a middle-of-the-road choice. Section 8.2.1.2 (physical pp. 108--109) caps the attack at 2^80, 2^120, and 2^160 stored j-invariants.

Validated finite-size model

The new review validates the attack's two main finite-size ingredients. Isogenies from E to its Frobenius conjugate of degree below p/4 lie in a rank-three lattice whose degree-form determinant is p/4. The expected count through degree D is

(4*pi/(3*zeta(3))) * D^(3/2) / sqrt(p)
  = 3.484685... * D^(3/2) / sqrt(p).

Exact Sage experiments at all three submitted primes match this count within about five percent at the well-populated radii. They also reproduce the (p/2)^(1/3) upper bound from Aubry, Oyono and Vincent; the measured median shortest degree is 0.39--0.44 times that bound.

The success model counts every way that a short degree can split between the two smooth-degree tables, including the measured multiplication-table loss. Across the nonzero p324_3 and p500_27 test points, its predicted success probability agrees with exact conjugate-isogeny samples within about 0.5 bit, with mixed sign. Small real-isogeny toy runs give similar agreement. These tests cover the free-memory operating region. At the submitted memory caps, the relevant maximum degrees are about 28, 47 and 62 bits below the natural lattice radius and probe rare tails beyond the sampled range. The capped results therefore retain that extrapolation. The p664_17 end-to-end validation timed out, adding a separate Level-V gap.

Concrete costs

The table gives base-2 work exponents. “Specification” charges each generated table entry log2(B)^2 log2(p)^2 bit operations, as in Remark 8.2.1. “One Fp2 multiplication” charges the explicit circuit cost of one field multiplication per entry; it is a sensitivity convention, not a lower bound on full entry generation. log2(p)^2 is a still more optimistic per-entry sensitivity. The free-memory shortfall is therefore 5.50, 7.02 and 15.83--16.16 bits. It is now supported by lattice and success-probability experiments rather than a direct p/3 exponent substitution.

The Level-I result at the submitted cap depends on entry generation cost. The attack crosses the 143-bit target if an entry costs only log2(p)^2 bit operations, while charging one Fp2 multiplication leaves 0.28--0.47 bit and the specification's Cantor--Zassenhaus analysis leaves 7.69 bits. At the optimized Level-I point, reaching the category boundary requires roughly 2^19.4 bit operations per entry. Known root-finding and field arithmetic are more expensive; the specification uses approximately 2^27.3. This is a sensitivity boundary, not an implemented below-target attack.

The specification says its more detailed analysis costs at least 3*sqrt(p/w) generated j-invariants before deliberately using the weaker sqrt(p/w) expression. The experiments support the square-root scaling but measure constants around 1.4--2.4 in the tested region rather than reproducing the factor three. They do not reach the rare-tail cap regime and therefore do not disprove the specification's stronger constant. The optimized table above uses the measured success model and records this extrapolation explicitly.

What changed from the earlier report

Wesolowski's rough §4.1 calculation is not a lower bound on attack work. Once the lattice density and all smooth-degree splits are included, its free-memory log2(p)^2 convention gives about 126--128, 187--189 and 243--244 bits, not the earlier 152, 221 and 283-bit conversion. It cannot be used as an above-target defence. Memory cost is the remaining reason that the validated time estimates do not establish a NIST-category failure.

Ouyang, Wei and Xu prove the relevant smoothness law asymptotically, and Delgado gives an independent unconditional Las Vegas algorithm. These remove the original asymptotic heuristic while retaining p^(1/3+o(1)) memory. Mamah's concrete analysis concerns earlier SQIsign primes; its table cannot simply be relabelled with the On-Ramp parameters.

Van Oorschot--Wiener collision search is at least 16 bits slower in the reviewed model. Pollard rho, dissection and asymmetric degree sets give no improvement. The optimistic quantum attacks also remain above their NIST depth-limited benchmarks.

Classification and reproduction

This remains Low / Lead. The finite-size free-memory attack model is now well supported, but all three attacks require roughly 2^110 to 2^224 table entries. NIST permits category comparisons to charge access to enormous memory, and the illustrative w^(1/3) sensitivity charge puts every set above target. It is not a prescribed NIST metric. No full-scale attack was executed, the capped rare-tail extrapolation is unvalidated, and the Level-V success check remains open. An accepted below-target area-time model or practical entry-generation method would justify reassessing the severity.
