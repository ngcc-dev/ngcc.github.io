MQOM-3 — Low: correlated-tree standard-MQ bound falls 19–49 bits short

Proof scope

MQOM's security discussion (§5.1, physical pp.73–78) first gives a formal EUF-KO theorem common to both tree variants. It then states that EUF-CMA follows by simulating the signing oracle. For the correlated-tree variant, that step is given by the external FR26 result and assumes partial-guessing one-wayness of MQ (PGOW-MQ). For the one-tree variant, the specification asserts (physical p.77), without a written proof, bound or reference, that a simpler reduction relies directly on standard MQ. Thus the specification does discuss EUF-CMA; the gap reported here is confined to the correlated-tree assumption chain.

The specification calls PGOW-MQ weaker than standard MQ because the adversary has a prefix-validation oracle. Section 5.3.2, physical pp.90–91, gives a non-tight reduction from PGOW-MQ to standard MQ. Table 26 (physical p.91) reports these lower bounds:

Field Category Table 26 lower bound Submission target Gap
GF(2) 1 122 143 21
GF(2) 3 188 207 19
GF(2) 5 245 272 27
GF(16) 1 121 143 22
GF(16) 3 171 207 36
GF(16) 5 223 272 49

Every one of the nine correlated-tree sets maps to one of these field/category rows, so the standard-MQ reduction alone certifies a lower bound 19–49 bits below the submission's adopted NIST gate-count target. The specification describes the reduction's loss relative to its full-MQ estimates as 20–65 bits; that is a different comparison from the target gaps tabulated above. Using the selected Crossbred rows in Table 24 for GF(2) and WXL rows in Table 23 for GF(16), that loss is 22, 25 and 37 bits for GF(2) and 44.6, 64.1 and 79.1 bits for GF(16) at Categories 1, 3 and 5. These are selected cost comparisons, not the minimum of every listed full-MQ algorithm: PXL is 3.9 bits cheaper than WXL for GF(16) Category 1, and Dinur 1.9 is 1 and 4 bits cheaper than Crossbred for GF(2) Categories 3 and 5, respectively, under its much larger memory requirement. The specification's "20 to 65" understates the selected GF(16) Category 5 row (302.1 − 223 = 79.1).

Classification and limits

This is a Low proof gap, not an attack. The calculation does not give an algorithm meeting a Table 26 lower bound. Section 5.3.3 separately analyzes PGOW-MQ directly and argues that its validation oracle is unlikely to provide a significant practical advantage. Those estimates support the parameter rationale but do not turn the non-tight standard-MQ reduction into a proof at the claimed targets.

The nine one-tree sets are outside this report because their stated EUF-CMA reduction relies directly on standard MQ without the PGOW-MQ step. Closing the finding requires a tighter reduction, a sufficiently supported direct PGOW-MQ hardness result at each target, or use of the one-tree construction, whose direct-MQ reduction the specification asserts but does not write out.

Reproduction

python3 MQOM/reproduce_mqom_3.py

The deterministic standard-library script pins the specification, recomputes the six target gaps from Table 26, and checks parameters.csv to confirm that all nine -ct sets and no -ot sets are in scope. It is a table/model check, not a cryptanalytic attack.

Harness reproduction

From the ngcc-harness checkout, run the pinned source script through the harness-side dispatcher:

python3 nist/reproduce.py MQOM-3
