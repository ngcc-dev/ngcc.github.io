UOV-5 — Low: the formal EUF-CMA theorem does not cover the recommended signer

Scope mismatch

The specification states in §4, physical p.22, that no formal reduction from a well-known hard problem has been presented for UOV. It then points to Appendix A for the EUF-CMA argument for salt-UOV. That construction fixes a random vinegar vector and resamples the salt until the linear system is solvable.

The recommended signer in Figure 2 and the submitted ov.c does the opposite: it samples one salt, derives vinegar from the message, salt, secret seed and an attempt counter, and retries with new vinegar until its secret linear system is invertible. The specification argues that known attacks apply to both variants, but that is not a reduction for the recommended signing distribution.

Quantitative limit

Appendix-A Theorem 1 gives the salt-UOV reduction numerator

1 - (q_h + q_s) * q_s * 2^-128.

At the NIST evaluation budget q_s=2^64, this is already zero when q_h=0 and negative for any positive hash-query budget. The theorem also loses a factor q_h+q_s+1. These observations concern only what the theorem certifies; they do not yield an attack on either signer.

Run the retained source and arithmetic certificate with:

python3 UOV/reproduce_uov_5.py

It pins the specification and submitted signer, confirms the fixed-salt, retry-vinegar source order, and evaluates the theorem numerator at the NIST signing-query budget. Because a proof for a related signer cannot establish the required EUF-CMA claim for the submitted signer, this is a Low Proof gap. A remedy is a reduction covering Figure 2's exact distribution and realistic query budgets, or adoption of a signing procedure covered by an applicable proof.
