UOV-5 — Low: the formal EUF-CMA theorem does not cover the recommended signer

Scope mismatch

The specification states in §4, physical p.22, that no formal reduction from a well-known hard problem has been presented for UOV. It then points to Appendix A for the EUF-CMA argument for salt-UOV. That construction fixes a random vinegar vector and resamples the salt until the linear system is solvable.

The recommended signer in Figure 2 and the submitted ov.c does the opposite: it samples one salt, derives vinegar from the message, salt, secret seed and an attempt counter, and retries with new vinegar until its secret linear system is invertible. The specification argues that known attacks apply to both variants, but that is not a reduction for the recommended signing distribution.

Theorem 1 is quoted from Sakumoto, Shirai and Hiwatari (PQCrypto 2011). Kosuge and Xagawa ("Probabilistic Hash-and-Sign with Retry in the Quantum Random Oracle Model", §4.2 and Appendix B.2) state that this proof's random-oracle simulation is flawed, crediting Chatterjee, Das and Pandit, and give a corrected bound; the specification cites neither. Their Proposition 3 treats original UOV with fresh, independently sampled vinegar on each retry, and the authors note applicability to the then-current NIST UOV submission. The pinned Round 3 signer instead derives each vinegar deterministically from message, salt, sk_seed and attempt counter. Applying the proposition to these exact source-level coins needs an additional argument. The proposition also relies on a computational preimage-simulatability assumption for which the authors say no statistical bound is known and which they leave unproven.

Quantitative limit

Appendix-A Theorem 1 gives the salt-UOV reduction numerator

1 - (q_h + q_s) * q_s * 2^-128.

At the NIST evaluation budget q_s=2^64, this is already zero when q_h=0 and negative for any positive hash-query budget. The theorem also loses a factor q_h+q_s+1. Because salt_len = 128 at every category (Table 4, p.21), the numerator is also negative for a single signing query once q_h >= 2^128, a hash budget inside the Category 3 and 5 targets of uov-III and uov-V. At q_h = 2^96 it stays above 1/2 only for q_s below about 2^31. With a 128-bit salt, the additive term of the Kosuge–Xagawa proposition, (3/2) q_s sqrt((q_s + q_ro + 1) / 2^128), is 2^32.58 at q_s = 2^64 for all four sets and reaches one at q_s = 2^42.28. These observations concern only what the theorem certifies; they do not yield an attack on either signer.

Run the retained source and arithmetic certificate with:

python3 UOV/reproduce_uov_5.py

It pins the specification and submitted signer, confirms the fixed-salt, retry-vinegar source order, evaluates the theorem numerator at the NIST signing-query budget and at the hash budgets above, and evaluates the Kosuge–Xagawa additive term. Because a proof for a related signer cannot establish the required EUF-CMA claim for the submitted signer, this is a Low Proof gap. A remedy is a reduction covering Figure 2's exact distribution and realistic query budgets, or adoption of a signing procedure covered by an applicable proof.
