HAWK-2 — Low: rejected signing attempts expose secret-derived control flow

Behavior

The signer regenerates the secret basis and enters an unbounded attempt loop at hawk_sign.c:904. It derives the secret coset parity from the basis and public hash at lines 955-977, then passes that parity to the constant-time Gaussian sampler at lines 987-1012. Although the sampler itself scans its tables with fixed bounds, the caller restarts when the sampled norm is too large at lines 1019-1021.

The signer next computes s1 using the secret basis. It exits the coefficient loop early and restarts if a bound is exceeded (1111-1131). Finally, Golomb–Rice encoding performs a data-dependent unary loop (679-754) and the signer restarts when the encoded candidate does not fit (1142-1158). Only the successful salt and s1 are returned. Therefore the count and work profile of rejected attempts are not determined by the final public signature.

The signer also regenerates (f,g) from the compact key after the s1-bound and encoding-overflow retries (hawk_sign.c:1128,1156), increasing the work distinguishing those retry paths.

The specification claims in §1.1.1 (p.4) that functions depending on secret data are isochronous. Section 3.5.1 (p.28) notes the sampler's constant-time table design, and §4.1.3 (p.39) specifically forbids an early exit from the secret table scan. The submitted sampler follows that rule. The surrounding rejection trace nevertheless processes secret-derived intermediates and is observable in principle to a local timer, branch tracer, or physical observer.

Impact and severity

The finding confirms secret-derived signing control flow at source level. It does not establish a statistically stable same-public-input channel, correlation with the long-term basis, or an extraction algorithm. It is unrelated to and strictly weaker than the public-key-only recovery in HAWK-1.

The specification reports restart probabilities of approximately 5.3e-6 for HAWK-512 and 2.5e-6 for HAWK-1024 (§3.5.2, pp.29-30), dominated by encoding failures. Its §6.3.3 proof also treats the restart predicate on a candidate signature as publicly checkable and simulates the restarting signing oracle from a non-restarting one. That proof argument does not establish that a local timing trace is key-independent, but it materially limits what can be inferred from the mere existence of retries.

The direct consequence boundary is Low under the classification methodology in security/methodology.md (security/methodology.md): this is source-level secret dependence without a measured channel or recovery map. Strict removal would require fixed-work batching and mask-selection among complete signing attempts, a material implementation change, but remediation cost alone does not raise the severity. Unlike QR-UOV's finer pivot trace, the observed predicate here is treated by the HAWK proof as publicly checkable and simulatable. No primary NIST security property is shown to fail.

Reproduction

python3 HAWK/reproduce_hawk_2.py
python3 HAWK/survey_runtime.py

The first command pins the three identical reference signer copies and the specification by SHA-256, then checks that the relevant source statements occur in the expected order. PASS denotes that source certificate only. It does not trace execution or claim a timing measurement, key recovery, or forgery.

The functional runtime screen confirms normal signatures verify, deterministic KAT-seed replay works, different seeds produce different keys, consecutive signatures differ, malformed lengths are rejected, and tested one-bit mutations do not produce an accepted signed message. Those functional results are compatible with the execution-trace finding.

Remediation and follow-up

A hardened implementation must use fixed-work attempt batching and constant-time selection of a successful complete encoding, with an explicitly bounded residual failure probability. Merely making encode_sig branchless does not hide the number of rejected candidates. Blinding or a platform isolation boundary may reduce observability but does not make the source trace constant-time.

A follow-up should instrument attempt causes and timing across many independently generated keys, control for public message and final signature features, and test whether the trace distinguishes or recovers any property of the basis.
