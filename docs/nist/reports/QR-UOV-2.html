QR-UOV-2 — Low: EUF-CMA reduction does not cover NIST's 2^64-query estimate

Bound and NIST baseline

QR-UOV's Theorem 2 contains the reduction factor (1 - qsign * epsilon_rank)^-1 and assumes qsign * epsilon_rank < 1/2 (specification §6.2, physical pp.36–38). Here epsilon_rank is a worst-case-over-keys rank-rejection probability. The specification does not prove that worst-case bound. It instead models the secret-structured signing matrix as a uniform random matrix and obtains

epsilon_rank approximately 1.173e-19 < 2^-62.88.

Even under that additional heuristic, the specification states that the condition is supported only when signature generations per key are limited to qsign <= 2^61. It expressly says the calculation is not a worst-case bound.

NIST's call §4.B.2, physical p.14, says that for estimating security strength the attacker may be assumed to obtain signatures on no more than 2^64 chosen messages. Applying QR-UOV's own uniform-matrix estimate gives

2^61 * epsilon_rank = 0.270431... < 1/2
2^64 * epsilon_rank = 2.163448... > 1

Thus the theorem's required premise already fails before the full NIST query ceiling. At 2^64, even the displayed denominator is no longer a meaningful positive reduction factor.

Independent QROM loss

Theorem 2 also contains the additive term

(3/2) * qsign' * sqrt((qsign' + 3*qsign + qqro + 5) / 2^lambda).

Here qsign' counts the random-oracle evaluations made during signing, so it is at least qsign. Even in the favorable case qsign'=qsign and qqro=0, this term alone is approximately 2^33.58, 2^1.58, and 2^-30.42 at qsign=2^64 for lambda=128, 192, and 256, respectively. It reaches one at about 2^41.61 signing queries for category 1 and 2^62.94 for category 3. The theorem's bound is therefore vacuous before the NIST ceiling for those two categories independently of the rank-probability issue.

Classification and limits

This is a candidate-specific proof and usage-bound shortfall, rated Low / Proof gap under the project methodology. It is not an attack: the calculation neither proves that the structured matrices attain the heuristic failure probability nor turns rank failures into a forgery. It does not show that any QR-UOV parameter has fewer than its claimed security bits in practice. Conversely, the absence of a worst-case bound means the theorem is not fully established even within the proposed 2^61 operational limit.

Closing the report requires a suitable per-key usage rule accepted as part of the security target, parameters that keep both losses meaningful through 2^64, or a proof that avoids or rigorously bounds the rank-rejection and QROM losses.

Reproduction

python3 QR-UOV/reproduce_qruov_2.py

The deterministic script evaluates the specification's finite-field random-matrix formula for every main-set value of m, checks the pinned specification, compares the products at 2^61 and 2^64, and evaluates the first additive QROM term for all three security parameters. This is a cost/model check of the stated theorem, not a security reduction or attack.

Harness reproduction

From the ngcc-harness checkout, run the pinned source script through the harness-side dispatcher:

python3 nist/reproduce.py QR-UOV-2
