QR-UOV-4 — Low: zero-guess Hashimoto–PXL composition conditionally falls below two targets

Result

QR-UOV specification §7.2, physical pp.42–43, estimates Polynomial XL (PXL) after the Thomae–Wolf reduction. It says that PXL is difficult to combine with the stronger partition-based underdetermined solvers because both guess variables before substituting their values. That explanation does not cover Hashimoto's zero-guess boundary.

In Ostuzzi's geometric formulation, Hashimoto extraction with k_H=0 and partition parameter b leaves a square MQ(m-b-1,m-b-1) residual. No variable value has been guessed at this point, so PXL can make its own symbolic choice of variables on the residual. Substitution in Ostuzzi's equation (7), the original Hashimoto condition, and Asanuma et al.'s restatement gives:

Parameter set Original (n,m) b Hashimoto residual Thomae–Wolf residual
1q127L10 (600,60) 10 49 51
5q127L10 (1150,110) 9 100 101
3q127L3 (306,78) 2 75 76
5q31L10 (1240,120) 9 110 111

Applying the specification's affine Hilbert-series formula, its gate conversion, and the PXL paper's asymptotic omega=2.37 matrix exponent gives:

Parameter set Recomputed TW→PXL Hashimoto(0)→PXL Target Margin
1q127L10 148.64 141.95 143 -1.05
5q127L10 272.98 270.05 272 -1.95
3q127L3 210.58 208.74 207 +1.74
5q31L10 275.45 273.90 272 +1.90

The centrifugation and initial square-system costs are below 49 bits for the two affected sets in the same model and do not change the displayed totals. The result corrects the submission's chosen estimator: under that estimator, the stated reason for using only Thomae–Wolf before PXL misses two below-target rows.

Those figures price one residual solve. If each conditioned square residual has the independent random-system success probability 1-e^-1, repeated reductions add about log2(1/(1-e^-1)) = 0.66 bit: approximately 142.61 and 270.71 bits. This is a heuristic sensitivity, not a measured full-size success rate. The ten small structured toys needed an average of 2.4 target attempts; they do not establish that unsuccessful full-size residuals can be screened cheaply before PXL. At Level I the restart-adjusted margin is only about 0.39 bit, so small omitted costs could erase it.

Why this remains a lead

The figures are not practical finite-size attack costs. They depend on three material assumptions:

  1. omega=2.37 is an asymptotic existence bound. Neither the PXL paper, the submission, nor this study supplies an algorithm or crossover showing that it applies to these dense matrices at dimensions 49 and 100. Replacing it by the PXL paper's omega=2.81 sensitivity gives 148.24 and 288.33 bits, both above target.
  2. The modeled PXL storage is about 2^110.27 and 2^211.15 field elements, or 2^113.08 and 2^213.96 bits. A specific logarithmic memory-access charge gives about 148.73 and 277.77 bits. This is a sensitivity model; the size of a defensible access charge has not been fixed by the toy runs.
  3. PXL assumes suitable semi-regular behavior. Five retained Sage trials validate zero-guess centrifugation and a lifted forgery for random quadratic maps over GF(31). A separate dependency-free toy constructs the submitted q=31, ell=3 quotient-ring public-map distribution at reduced dimensions (V,M)=(3,2). Seeds 20261018 through 20261027 all produced the predicted 4-by-4 residual, Hilbert function [1,4,6,4,1,0], and a valid lifted forgery. This is useful structured evidence, but it does not establish semi-regularity at dimensions 49 or 100 and uses brute-force residual solving rather than PXL. The specification's Table 13 experiments likewise concern the original QR-MQ system rather than the conditioned residual.

The alternative CryptographicEstimators implementation gives lower numbers under a different homogeneous convention. It is a sensitivity check, not independent confirmation of the specification's affine calculation.

This is therefore Low / Lead under the project's security-margin rule. It is not a demonstrated category break or full-scale forgery. If full-size structured-residual tests and a realizable finite-size cost establish a below-target path, the direct EUF-CMA consequence would require a substantially higher severity assessment.

Other parameter observations

Recalculation of the submission and Round-2 tables shows that the previously reported thinnest published QR-UOV margin is 5q127L10 at 273 bits, one bit above its 272-bit target. The q=7 and q=31 sets remain above their targets under the specification's formula; another estimator puts 5q31L10 at 271.9 under a different convention, so that value is not presented as a separate attack.

The characteristic-two divided-square row-selection mechanism studied for UOV does not transfer directly to odd-characteristic QR-UOV. Higher characteristic-p divided-power methods exist, but no sparse-row gain for the submitted QR-UOV sets was established here.

Reproduction

python3 QR-UOV/reproduce_qruov_4.py

The dependency-free script checks the two Hashimoto applicability inequalities, residual dimensions, specification-formula PXL optima, omitted preprocessing cost, memory units, and omega=2.81 sensitivity. It is a deterministic cost/model check, not PXL execution.

The optional mechanism tests are:

python3 QR-UOV/analysis/hashimoto_pxl/structured_toy.py --trials 10
sage QR-UOV/analysis/hashimoto_pxl/toy_hashimoto.sage

The first command constructs small quotient-ring QR-UOV maps without external packages. The second uses random MQ maps. Neither runs PXL or validates the full-size cost.

Harness reproduction

From the ngcc-harness checkout, run the pinned source script through the harness-side dispatcher:

python3 nist/reproduce.py QR-UOV-4
